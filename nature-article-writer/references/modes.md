# Structural modes are editorial aids

The bundled article and letter templates are starting structures, not authoritative
journal schemas or proof that a publication accepts that content type today.
Use the current journal's guide to select a submission format. The old generic
`portfolio-article`/`portfolio-letter` preflight profiles have been removed.

For a full research article, an abstract plus Introduction/Results/Discussion/Methods
can help organise the evidence. For a concise narrative, a connected sequence of
claims may work better. Main Nature Articles use a referenced summary paragraph;
other journals can require an unreferenced abstract. Choose from the verified
journal contract, not from the perceived prestige or length of the paper.

Keep section headings when they help readers and the format permits them. Choose
one term consistently for the opening. Do not add an artificial 'Summary paragraph'
heading to the final submission merely to satisfy this skill's optional checker;
use a separate text extract with `--opening-only` instead.

The useful questions are: what does the opening need to communicate, what evidence
supports each section, what order makes the argument clear, and what end matter
is actually applicable? The title and word limits, number of figures, heading
policy, and required declarations come from dated journal-specific evidence.

See [journal calibration](journal-calibration.md) for the actual length-check
profiles and their deliberately limited scope.
