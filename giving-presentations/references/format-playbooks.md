# Format Playbooks

Use this reference when the user names a specific presentation format or when the format implies different structure, pacing, or evidence.

## Keynote or conference talk

Goal: Make one idea memorable and emotionally resonant.

Structure:

1. Vivid opening story, image, or surprising truth.
2. Name the audience’s current world.
3. Show what is broken, changing, or misunderstood.
4. Introduce the core insight.
5. Build proof through stories, examples, and selective data.
6. Give the audience a new model or language.
7. Show what becomes possible.
8. End with a callback and memorable final line.

Design notes:

- Sparse slides with strong visuals.
- More story and contrast than bullet lists.
- Rehearse opening and close tightly.
- State changes every 3–5 minutes.

## Scientific or academic talk

Goal: Help the audience understand the question, trust the method, interpret the result, and see the implication.

Structure:

1. Broad context and why the problem matters.
2. Gap in current knowledge or capability.
3. Research question or hypothesis.
4. Method overview with just enough detail.
5. Key result 1 with interpretation.
6. Key result 2 with interpretation.
7. Controls, uncertainty, limitations.
8. Mechanism or model.
9. Implications and next steps.
10. Clear takeaway.

Design notes:

- Use a bowtie: broad → narrow → broad.
- Do not bury the contribution in methods.
- Label figures with “what you see” and “what it means.”
- Put details in backup for specialist questions.
- State limitations confidently; it builds credibility.

## Technical briefing

Goal: Enable a technical or cross-functional audience to understand status, risk, and next engineering decision.

Structure:

1. Decision/context.
2. System overview.
3. Current state and constraint.
4. Evidence or diagnostics.
5. Options.
6. Recommendation.
7. Risks and mitigations.
8. Next engineering steps.

Design notes:

- Use diagrams before dense implementation detail.
- Define terms early.
- Put assumptions close to the claim they affect.
- Separate “known,” “unknown,” and “assumed.”

## Product demo

Goal: Make the audience believe the product solves a meaningful user problem.

Structure:

1. Name the user and the job-to-be-done.
2. Show the painful current workflow.
3. Introduce the product promise.
4. Demo the shortest path to value.
5. Highlight the non-obvious capability.
6. Show result or impact.
7. Explain why it is credible/scalable.
8. Ask for next step.

Design notes:

- Do not tour menus.
- Use realistic data.
- Keep setup short.
- Narrate value, not clicks.
- Have screenshots/video backup in case the live demo fails.

## Sales pitch

Goal: Move the buyer from pain recognition to confident next step.

Structure:

1. Buyer-specific problem.
2. Cost of status quo.
3. New insight about the problem.
4. Solution framed around outcomes.
5. Proof: case study, demo, quantified impact.
6. Differentiation.
7. Implementation path.
8. Risk reversal.
9. Clear next step.

Design notes:

- Use the buyer’s language.
- Minimize generic company slides.
- Put ROI in context.
- Prepare procurement, security, and implementation questions.

## Fundraising pitch

Goal: Create belief in the market, team, insight, traction, and right to win.

Structure:

1. What you do in plain language.
2. Non-obvious insight or market shift.
3. User pain and urgency.
4. Product and why now.
5. Traction/proof.
6. Market size and wedge.
7. Business model.
8. Competition and differentiation.
9. Team.
10. Ask and use of funds.

Design notes:

- Avoid a deck that tries to convince every possible skeptic.
- Lead with clarity and earned insight.
- Be ready to go deep on metrics, market, and defensibility.
- Keep the spoken pitch shorter than the meeting to leave room for discussion.

## Board update

Goal: Enable governance, advice, and decisions.

Structure:

1. Executive summary and asks.
2. What changed since last meeting.
3. Performance against plan.
4. Key risks/opportunities.
5. Strategic decision or discussion topic.
6. Function-specific deep dives only as needed.
7. Decisions, follow-ups, and owners.

Design notes:

- Highlight changes, surprises, and decisions.
- Do not make the board infer the ask.
- Put supporting detail in appendices.
- Address bad news directly with response plan.

## All-hands update

Goal: Align, motivate, and clarify priorities.

Structure:

1. Human opening or customer story.
2. What matters now.
3. Progress and wins.
4. Challenges named honestly.
5. Priorities for the next period.
6. What teams should do differently.
7. Recognition and close.

Design notes:

- Translate strategy into what it means for people’s work.
- Avoid executive jargon.
- Use stories and concrete examples.
- Leave space for questions.

## Lecture or teaching session

Goal: Help learners understand and apply a concept.

Structure:

1. Why this matters.
2. What learners will be able to do.
3. Prerequisite concepts.
4. Concept 1 with example.
5. Quick check or activity.
6. Concept 2 with example.
7. Practice/application.
8. Common mistakes.
9. Summary and next practice.

Design notes:

- Segment the material.
- Use worked examples.
- Reduce extraneous information.
- Ask learners to do something, not just listen.

## Webinar

Goal: Teach or persuade a distributed audience while maintaining attention.

Structure:

1. Clear promise and who it is for.
2. Agenda with outcomes.
3. Poll or chat prompt early.
4. Main content in short modules.
5. Interaction every 5–7 minutes.
6. Summary and action.
7. Q&A.

Design notes:

- Use more signposting than in-person talks.
- Keep slides visually simple.
- Use chat, polls, and named transitions.
- Have a moderator if possible.
- Repeat questions before answering.

## Lightning talk

Goal: Land one idea fast.

Structure:

1. Hook.
2. One problem.
3. One insight.
4. One example.
5. One implication.
6. One ask or memorable close.

Design notes:

- Cut background aggressively.
- One slide per beat, or no slides.
- No complex charts.
- Practice timing tightly.

## Workshop or interactive session

Goal: Move people from understanding to doing.

Structure:

1. Outcome and why it matters.
2. Short concept.
3. Demonstration.
4. Individual practice.
5. Pair/group discussion.
6. Debrief.
7. Repeat for next concept.
8. Commitments or next actions.

Design notes:

- Design activities before slides.
- Give clear instructions and timing.
- Use examples of good outputs.
- Keep lecture segments short.

## Async recorded presentation

Goal: Communicate clearly without live feedback.

Structure:

1. Up-front summary.
2. Clear navigation.
3. Short sections.
4. Explicit transitions.
5. More visual annotations.
6. Recap after each section.
7. Clear next step.

Design notes:

- Avoid relying on live charisma.
- Keep videos shorter than live talks.
- Add captions.
- Provide a written summary and links.
